How we work

How we stay legal

In the UK, a business supplying a disc containing a film generally needs a BBFC rating for it, under the Video Recordings Act 1984, unless the film or the supply is exempt. Here's how we work within that.

Route A: Disc Lab (studio session or at-home kit)

We supply mastering as a digital disc file, artwork, blank printed discs (nothing recorded on them), packaging and teaching. You, or someone you nominate who isn't connected to Normal Shoes and isn't paid to burn, make the copies. We charge per session, mastering package and blank media; never per finished copy. A maximum of 10 discs per session, for personal use.

Route B: rated releases and compilations

Your film is classified by the BBFC first. Then we can manufacture and sell finished discs to anyone. Rated releases.

Route C: event keepsakes

Finished discs of recordings of real events, such as weddings, shows, graduations and festival award nights, supplied only to people who took part and their families, with no strong sexual or violent content. Event keepsakes.

Why we ask what your film is

The route depends on what's on the disc and who it's for. So we ask what your film is, who it's for, and whether you own the rights, including the music, before we start.

What we never do

We never supply finished discs of unrated films to anyone. We don't sell discs of festival films to audiences, and we never charge per finished copy of a film.

When we're unsure

If we're not sure which route applies, we get the film rated.

This page explains how we work. It isn't legal advice.
